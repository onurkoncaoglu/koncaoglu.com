How to solve crown logic puzzles

In a crown puzzle (also known as a queens puzzle or star battle) every row, every column and every color region needs exactly one crown, and crowns may never touch, not even diagonally. Every well-made puzzle has exactly one solution that you can reach without guessing. These six techniques solve almost every board, from 5×5 to 10×10.

In the diagrams, thin X are marks you already have; bold X and crowns with a white ring are what the technique gives you.

Technique 1

A crown rules out its whole neighborhood

Once a crown is placed, nothing else can go in its row, its column, its color region or any of the eight cells around it. Mark all of them with X right away: the board becomes much smaller.

Technique 2

The last free cell takes the crown

When a row, column or region has only one cell left without an X, the crown must go there. Look for these after every move: they chain into each other.

Technique 3

A region inside one row (or column)

If all cells of a region lie in a single row, that region's crown is that row's crown. Every other cell of the row gets an X. The same works for a region inside a single column.

Technique 4

Two regions locked in two rows

Find a group of regions that fit inside the same number of rows: here the red and blue regions use only the top two rows, so they take both crowns of those rows. Every other region's cells in those rows are out. This works with three regions in three rows, and with columns too.

Technique 5

Cells every option can see

Take a small region and imagine the crown on each of its cells in turn. A cell that would be ruled out in every case can never hold a crown. Two-cell regions are the best place to start.

Technique 6

A row (or column) that only one region can fill

The reverse of technique 3: if the free cells of a column all belong to one region, that region's crown must be in that column. All of the region's cells outside the column get an X.

More tips

  • Start with the smallest regions and the shortest rows: they have the fewest options.
  • Mark X generously. In Royal Regions you can drag across cells to mark many at once.
  • Stuck? Count: if N regions fit inside N rows or columns, technique 4 always gives you something.
  • Never guess. If you seem to need a guess, there is a deduction you have not seen yet.
